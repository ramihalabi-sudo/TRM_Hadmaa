# Focal-plane-array (detector) and readout/electronics parameters: effect on EO/IR image quality and DRI range in TRM4 and NVIPM/TTP

Scope note for the report writer: these notes cover detector and ROIC/electronics parameters only (not optics, atmosphere, display or observer, except where they interact with detector parameters). Sources fetched or confirmed in this session are cited inline. Wherever I use standard radiometric or sampling relations that I did not re-verify in a source fetched in this session, I put them under **Inferences** and label them. Anything I could not source is under **Gaps**. Sources older than 2000 are marked *(foundational)*.

## 1. Parameter by parameter: physical definition, how it enters the range models, typical DRI impact, modern values

### Takeaway
Pixel pitch (through IFOV and Fλ/d), NETD (temporal noise at the operating F-number, integration time and well fill) and the detector MTF (aperture plus diffusion/crosstalk) are the detector quantities that enter range models directly. Residual fixed-pattern noise, spectral band, well capacity, read noise and dark current act through NETD or 3-D noise. Time constant, frame rate, bit depth, dead pixels, TDI and binning are second-order or scenario-dependent effects, and range models represent them only partly or implicitly.

### Cited Findings

**How detector and electronics quantities enter the two model families (overview)**
- TRM4 computes "the MTFs of all system components, the detected signal, and temporal and non-uniformity noise", lets the user specify every element of the chain (scene, atmosphere, optics, sensor, signal processing, display, observer), and uses the figures of merit MTDP (Minimum Temperature Difference Perceived) and MCP (Minimum Contrast Perceived). Ranges use modified Johnson criteria and the STANAG 4347 procedure. — [Fraunhofer IOSB, TRM4 product page (accessed 2026)](https://www.iosb.fraunhofer.de/en/projects-and-products/trm4.html)
- In TRM4 the sampling and spatial transfer of the imager are captured by AMOP ("Average Modulation at Optimum Phase") of a standard 4-bar pattern, which takes the place of the system MTF used in MRTD-based models. MTDP does not require all four bars to be resolved, so the model can handle undersampled FPAs. TRM4 generalises MTDP into MDSP for all imager categories. — [Keßler, Gal, Wittenstein, "TRM4: Range performance model for electro-optical imaging systems," Proc. SPIE 10178, 101780P (2017), DOI 10.1117/12.2262543](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/10178/101780P/TRM4-Range-performance-model-for-electro-optical-imaging-systems/10.1117/12.2262543.short); [ECOMOS TRM page](https://ecomos.org/information/trm/)
- The MTDP approach for undersampled thermal imagers goes back to Wittenstein's work in *Optical Engineering* (1999), DOI 10.1117/1.602265 *(foundational; cited as "original TRM approach" on the IOSB page)*. — [Fraunhofer IOSB TRM4 page](https://www.iosb.fraunhofer.de/en/projects-and-products/trm4.html)
- In NVIPM/TTP (the line of NVThermIP), the detector enters through the system MTF and through noise that raises the system contrast threshold function. The TTP metric was introduced in "New metric for predicting target acquisition performance" (Vollmerhausen, Jacobs, Driggers), Proc. SPIE 5076 (2003), DOI 10.1117/12.487215 (journal version Opt. Eng. 43(11), 2004). — [SPIE listing](https://neurophotonics.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/5076/0000/New-metric-for-predicting-target-acquisition-performance/10.1117/12.487215.full)
- NVIPM/TTP treats aliasing (sampling artefacts) as target-dependent noise, which is combined in quadrature with detector noise. The aliasing penalty shrinks with target contrast and grows with range, because sampling is fixed in angle space and targets become poorly sampled at long range. — [Vollmerhausen, Driggers, Wilson, "Predicting range performance of sampled imagers by treating aliased signal as target-dependent noise," JOSA A 25(8), 2055–2065 (2008) (erratum exists)](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/predicting-range-performance-of-sampled-imagers-by-treating-alias/); [Vollmerhausen, Reago, Driggers, *Analysis and Evaluation of Sampled Imaging Systems*, SPIE Press (2010), ch. 8 "Quantifying the effect of aliasing on visual task performance," DOI 10.1117/3.853462.ch8](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/ebooks/TT/Analysis-and-Evaluation-of-Sampled-Imaging-Systems/Chapter8/Quantifying-the-Effect-of-Aliasing-on-Visual-Task-Performance/10.1117/3.853462.ch8)
- A review of thermal-imager range prediction writes the classical relation as MRTD(f) = SNR·NETD/(MTF·F) and describes the 3-D (time, vertical, horizontal) noise model used for FPA systems. It assigns detection/recognition/identification pixel criteria of 2/7/13 pixels on target and Johnson 50 % criteria of 1/1.4/4/6.4 line pairs. — [Perić, Livada, Perić, Vujić, "Thermal Imager Range: Predictions, Expectations, and Reality," *Sensors* 19(15), 3313 (2019), DOI 10.3390/s19153313](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6696215/)

**Pixel pitch, array format, IFOV**
- Driggers et al. study target-discrimination range as a function of detector pitch for MWIR and LWIR. Their abstract says the benefit of smaller pixels is limited when the system is largely diffraction-limited, or when the small detector collects too few photons (noise-limited). It also notes that 12 µm was becoming standard for MWIR/LWIR in 2012, 10 µm was being pursued for large-format persistent surveillance, and 5 µm LWIR had been made in limited quantities. — [Driggers, Vollmerhausen, Reynolds, Fanning, Holst, "Infrared detector size: how low should you go?" Opt. Eng. 51(6), 063202 (2012), DOI 10.1117/1.OE.51.6.063202](https://photonicsforenergy.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/optical-engineering/volume-51/issue-6/063202/Infrared-detector-size-how-low-should-you-go/10.1117/1.OE.51.6.063202.full); conference version [Proc. SPIE 8355, 83550O (2012), DOI 10.1117/12.919951](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/infrared-detector-size-how-low-should-you-go/)
- Holst & Driggers give a minimum useful detector size of about 2 µm (MWIR) and 5 µm (LWIR) with F/1 optics at high SNR. At lower SNR they give 3 µm (MWIR) and 6 µm (LWIR) with F/1.2. — [Holst & Driggers, "Small detectors in infrared system design," Opt. Eng. 51(9), 096401 (2012), DOI 10.1117/1.OE.51.9.096401](https://spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/Optical-Engineering/volume-51/issue-9/096401/Small-detectors-in-infrared-system-design/10.1117/1.OE.51.9.096401.pdf) (numbers taken from search-result abstract text, full text not retrieved); [AZ Regents listing](https://experts.azregents.edu/en/publications/small-detectors-in-infrared-system-design/)
- SCD (manufacturer) argues that cutting pitch at constant IFOV shortens focal length, optics and Dewar (lower SWaP). At constant system size, a larger format "improves the image resolution and therefore performance metrics such as the range for target DRI." — [Shkedy et al. (SCD), "10 µm pitch detectors for MWIR applications," OPTRO 2018](https://scdusa-ir.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/10µm-pitch-detectors-for-MWIR-applications_OPTRO-2018.pdf)
- Pitch is still shrinking. Lynred reports a full-MWIR T2SL SXGA (1280×1024) at 7.5 µm pitch, operating at 130 K, and names electrical/optical crosstalk and image stability as the main concerns for pitches down to 7.5 µm. — [Péré-Laperne et al. (Lynred/III-V Lab/CEA-LETI), "T2SL technology developments in MWIR band operating at a high temperature," OPTRO 2026](https://www.lynred.com/sites/default/files/2026-01/T2SL%20TECHNOLOGY%20DEVELOPMENTS%20IN%20MWIR%20BAND.pdf)
- Other pitch-optimisation studies (titles found, contents not retrieved): "Is there an optimum detector size for digital night vision goggles?" [Proc. SPIE 9820, 98200C (2016)](https://spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/9820/98200C/Is-there-an-optimum-detector-size-for-digital-night-vision/10.1117/12.2228529.full); "Detector size selection for long-range targeting performance in the extended shortwave infrared band" [Proc. SPIE 13045, 1304504 (2024)](https://neurophotonics.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/13045/1304504/Detector-size-selection-for-long-range-targeting-performance-in-the/10.1117/12.3012973.full).

**Detector MTF: pixel aperture, diffusion, crosstalk**
- SCD Blackbird 10 µm InSb crosstalk: 56 % of the light falling on a pixel's area is detected in that pixel, 9 % in each of the 4 nearest neighbours, 2 % in each diagonal neighbour, and <2 % in the next ring. The measured detector MTF is 0.4 "at half the Nyquist frequency" (gold-mask PSF and knife edge agree). For 10 µm XBn-InAsSb the MTF is 0.36 at the same frequency and QE is about 70 %. — [Shkedy et al., OPTRO 2018](https://scdusa-ir.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/10µm-pitch-detectors-for-MWIR-applications_OPTRO-2018.pdf). *Caution:* "half the Nyquist frequency" probably means Nyquist, i.e. half the sampling frequency (see Inferences).
- Lynred T2SL full-MWIR: MTF at Nyquist = 0.48 ± 0.01 for VGA 15 µm (knife edge) and about 0.40 for SXGA 7.5 µm (stated objective, still being improved). Lynred calls MTF an important figure of merit "as it accounts for the detector range." — [Péré-Laperne et al., OPTRO 2026](https://www.lynred.com/sites/default/files/2026-01/T2SL%20TECHNOLOGY%20DEVELOPMENTS%20IN%20MWIR%20BAND.pdf)
- SCD says XBn's shorter ("blue") MWIR band gives better atmospheric transmission and better (diffraction) MTF, and that both increase DRI range. — [Shkedy et al., OPTRO 2018](https://scdusa-ir.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/10µm-pitch-detectors-for-MWIR-applications_OPTRO-2018.pdf)

**NETD: definition and dependence on F-number, integration time and well fill**
- SCD defines NETD as the temporal-noise-equivalent temperature difference at a stated frame rate and F-number, calls it "one of the critical parameters," and normally quotes it at a median well fill. Measured values: Blackbird-1920 InSb, F/4, 77 K, 70 % well fill (2.0 Me⁻ mode): mean 22.5 mK, σ 2.5 mK. Blackbird-640 XBn, F/3, 150 K, 50 % well fill: mean 36 mK, σ 4 mK. Noise² is linear in signal, which shows the FPA is shot-noise-limited, and NETD is BLIP "even at low well fill." — [Shkedy et al., OPTRO 2018](https://scdusa-ir.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/10µm-pitch-detectors-for-MWIR-applications_OPTRO-2018.pdf)
- Datasheet NETD conditions differ from product to product: Blackbird 1920 is <25 mK with 2 Me⁻ capacitors at 70 % well fill (F/3 cold shield). Sparrow Blackbird 640 XBn is 28 mK at 50 % well fill. — [SCD Blackbird 1920 page](https://scdusa-ir.com/products/blackbird-1920); [SCD Sparrow Blackbird 640 datasheet](https://scdusa-ir.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Sparrow_Blackbird-640_updated-V2a.pdf)
- Uncooled: SCD's 12 µm VOx is specified as temporal NETD <50 mK at 25 °C, F/1, 60 Hz, 7.5–14 µm. Noise is about 2 digital levels, with 1 DL ≈ 25 mK under nominal conditions. — [Jakobson et al. (SCD), "Microbolometer for high-end commercial and defense applications in 12 µm pitch," IISW 2025](https://imagesensors.org/Past%20Workshops/2025%20Workshop/2025%20Papers/P28_Jakobson.pdf)
- An industry overview gives 35–50 mK as typical NETD for 12 µm microbolometer arrays at F/1.0. — [AT-Sensors, "Microbolometer: basics of the uncooled infrared detector"](https://www.at-sensors.com/measurement/measurement-parameters/microbolometer-basics/) (vendor overview, secondary)

**Quantum efficiency, spectral band (cut-on/cut-off)**
- 10 µm InSb (77 K): QE >80 %, dark current <0.8 pA (typical ~0.2 pA). XBn-InAsSb: equivalent EO performance at up to 150 K, but cut-off at 4.2 µm ("blue" MWIR only), QE about 70 %. On fast airborne platforms with short integration times, the narrow XBn band limits SNR, so SCD recommends full-band InSb there. — [Shkedy et al., OPTRO 2018](https://scdusa-ir.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/10µm-pitch-detectors-for-MWIR-applications_OPTRO-2018.pdf)
- Lynred T2SL covers the full MWIR with cut-off 5.1 ± 0.1 µm at 130 K: external QE about 70 %, mean dark current 1 pA/pixel. A bulk InAsSb alternative has a 4.2 µm cut-off. — [Péré-Laperne et al., OPTRO 2026](https://www.lynred.com/sites/default/files/2026-01/T2SL%20TECHNOLOGY%20DEVELOPMENTS%20IN%20MWIR%20BAND.pdf)
- InAs/GaSb T2SL LWIR has lower QE than MCT because of its small absorption coefficient. — [search-indexed SPIE/Korean-group results summarised in JEIT review context](https://jeit.ac.cn/en/article/doi/10.11999/JEIT260083) (secondary; see Gaps)
- Emissivity enters the thermal contrast: a 1 % emissivity change is roughly equivalent to 0.6 K (MWIR) or 0.9 K (LWIR) of apparent ΔT. — [Perić et al., Sensors 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6696215/)

**Well capacity / charge handling, read noise, ADC**
- Blackbird family ROIC: 0.18 µm CMOS with on-chip 13-bit dual-ramp column ADCs and ITR/IWR modes. Selectable integration capacitors ("well fill capacity / readout noise at 77 K") range from 0.3 Me⁻/60 e⁻ and 0.5 Me⁻/90 e⁻ through 2.0 Me⁻/330 e⁻ (260 e⁻ ITR) up to 4.0 Me⁻/800 e⁻. The 640 lists 0.9 Me⁻/230 e⁻ and 2.0 Me⁻/440 e⁻ (330 e⁻ ITR). Read noise is Johnson-noise dominated and rises by up to 30 % at 150 K (XBn). Non-linearity is <0.06 % of DR over 5–80 % well fill. — [Shkedy et al., OPTRO 2018, Table 2](https://scdusa-ir.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/10µm-pitch-detectors-for-MWIR-applications_OPTRO-2018.pdf) (the PDF table extraction made the per-format column assignment partly ambiguous; the 1920 NETD figure explicitly uses the 2.0 Me⁻ mode.)
- Lynred SXGA 7.5 µm T2SL ROIC: storable charge 0.41/0.7/1.1 Me⁻, linearity ±0.5 %, about 100 mW at 130 K, 60 Hz full frame, snapshot ITR/IWR, 2×2 binning and windowing. — [Péré-Laperne et al., OPTRO 2026](https://www.lynred.com/sites/default/files/2026-01/T2SL%20TECHNOLOGY%20DEVELOPMENTS%20IN%20MWIR%20BAND.pdf)
- SWIR InGaAs (SCD Cardinal 1280, 10 µm): QE >80 % at 1550 nm, dark current about 1 fA at 280 K (another SCD-linked source says ~0.5 fA), wells 12 ke⁻ (high gain) / 600 ke⁻ (low gain). — [SCD Cardinal 1280 page](https://scdusa-ir.com/products/cardinal-1280/) (values via search snippet). Read noise of other 10 µm 1280×1024 InGaAs cameras: Raptor Owl 1280 <50 e⁻ rms (69 dB intra-scene DR) — [Owl 1280 datasheet](https://www.photonicsonline.com/doc/high-resolution-high-sensitivity-digital-vis-swir-camera-owl1280-datasheet-0001); Quantum Imaging QI-SWIR-HD10 <40 e⁻ rms — [Vision Systems Design](https://vision-systems.com/cameras-accessories/article/16751788/highdefinition-swir-camera-developed-by-quantum-imaging); NIT SenS 1280 30 e⁻, 0.9–1.7 µm — [FindLight listing](https://findlight.net/imaging-and-vision/cameras/infrared-and-thermal/sens-1280v-st).
- SCD 12 µm VOx bolometer ROIC: column-parallel CTIA plus slope ADC, "13bit ADC" (abstract) with 14-bit LVCMOS output (table), in-pixel memory for averaging up to 2 frames, 25–120 Hz rolling shutter. — [Jakobson et al., IISW 2025](https://imagesensors.org/Past%20Workshops/2025%20Workshop/2025%20Papers/P28_Jakobson.pdf)

**Dark current and operating temperature (HOT)**
- XBn-InAsSb reaches InSb-equivalent EO performance at up to ~150 K, which cuts cooler power and raises MTTF. That makes it suited to 24/7 surveillance and low-SWaP UAV payloads. — [Shkedy et al., OPTRO 2018](https://scdusa-ir.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/10µm-pitch-detectors-for-MWIR-applications_OPTRO-2018.pdf)
- FPA operating temperatures on datasheets: Blackbird 1280 is 80 K (InSb) or 150 K (XBn), with RNU 0.04 % STD/DR over 10–80 % well fill. — [SCD Blackbird 1280 page](https://scdusa-ir.com/products/blackbird-1280/); [Mini Blackbird 1280 datasheet (XBn, 150 K, <25 mK at 70 % WF)](https://scdusa-ir.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Mini_Blackbird-1280_14-2-18_updated-V2a.pdf)
- MWIR T2SL review: dark-current densities generally below 1×10⁻⁴ A/cm², peak QE approaching 70 %. nBn devices reach about 2×10⁻⁵ A/cm² at 150 K, and later designs reach the 10⁻⁶ A/cm² level near 150 K. — [JEIT review "Research status and prospects of MWIR superlattice detector technology" (2025/26), DOI 10.11999/JEIT260083](https://jeit.ac.cn/en/article/doi/10.11999/JEIT260083). An InAs/InAsSb nBn array measured 4.57×10⁻⁵ A/cm² at 150 K, rising to 9×10⁻² A/cm² at 295 K. — [Acta Optica Sinica 45(7), 0712001 (2025)](https://www.researching.cn/articles/OJ9fe547442c425bbc)
- Rogalski & Martyniuk modelled MWIR HgCdTe nBn as a near-room-temperature alternative to HOT bulk HgCdTe and T2SL at 230 K. — [Bull. Pol. Acad. Sci. (2013)](https://journals.pan.pl//Content/83686/PDF/paper_20.pdf)

**Fixed-pattern noise, residual non-uniformity, 3-D noise**
- SCD defines RNU as the spatial STD left after a linear 2-point NUC (at 20 % and 80 % well fill). They distinguish global RNU from local RNU (STD over a 15×15 neighbourhood) and note that local, high-frequency non-uniformity "determines the ability of the detector to distinguish targets from their close environment." Blackbird: global <0.04 % and local <0.02 % STD/DR over a wide well-fill range, i.e. spatial noise below temporal noise ("for a good BLIP detector the spatial noise should be lower than the temporal noise"). NUC stability is shown after on/off and cooling cycles and over 0–50 °C ambient. — [Shkedy et al., OPTRO 2018](https://scdusa-ir.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/10µm-pitch-detectors-for-MWIR-applications_OPTRO-2018.pdf)
- Lynred T2SL VGA: RFPN/temporal-noise ratio stays <1 over the full blackbody range (their objective), and is stable over three cool-downs. — [Péré-Laperne et al., OPTRO 2026](https://www.lynred.com/sites/default/files/2026-01/T2SL%20TECHNOLOGY%20DEVELOPMENTS%20IN%20MWIR%20BAND.pdf)
- First perception experiment to compare FPN and temporal noise directly for target identification: static IR tank images at 6 FPN levels × 6 temporal-noise levels, 10 soldiers. — [Driggers, Vollmerhausen, Krapels, "Target identification performance as a function of temporal and fixed pattern noise," Opt. Eng. 40(3) (2001), DOI 10.1117/1.1346583](https://spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/optical-engineering/volume-40/issue-03/0000/Target-identification-performance-as-a-function-of-temporal-and-fixed/10.1117/1.1346583.full) (abstract only; the quantitative result was not retrieved)
- A 3-D noise analysis of a wide-FOV LWIR sensor (Haefner's MATLAB 3-D noise tools) found σtvh, σvh and σh-type components (column-correlated FPN, 2-D random spatial noise, temporal noise). FPN hurt dynamic MRT (DMRT) less than static MRT: by about 3× at mid spatial frequencies and less than 2× at low and high frequencies. The pilotage TTP metric degrades the eye CTF by a noise factor. — ["The impact of fixed pattern noise on moving imagery as measured by MRT and DMRT," Proc. SPIE 13468, 134680B (2025), DOI 10.1117/12.3054006](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/13468/134680B/The-impact-of-fixed-pattern-noise-on-moving-imagery-as/10.1117/12.3054006.full); follow-on ["Fixed-pattern noise impact on pilotage sensor performance," Applied Optics (2025), Lawrence et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40981899/)
- Current 3-D noise methodology: ["3D noise photon transfer curve," Applied Optics 61(21), 6202 (2022)](https://opg.optica.org/ao/abstract.cfm?URI=ao-61-21-6202); ["Measurements and metrics of fixed pattern noise: a new procedure for thermal camera noise measurement," Proc. SPIE 12533, 125330T (2023)](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/12533/125330T/Measurements-and-metrics-of-fixed-pattern-noise--a-new/10.1117/12.2663353.short); ["Power spectral density of 3D noise measurements," SPIE (2017)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/316713807_Power_spectral_density_of_3D_noise_measurements)

**Microbolometer time constant (smear), frame rate**
- Typical microbolometer thermal time constant is 7–15 ms, which in practice caps useful frame rates at about 30–60 Hz. — [AT-Sensors overview](https://www.at-sensors.com/measurement/measurement-parameters/microbolometer-basics/). A broader range of about 2–25 ms is given in [US patent 11,368,637](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11368637).
- SCD 12 µm VOx: τ <7 ms with NETD <50 mK, reported as an excellent NETD×τ figure of merit. Operability >99.5 %, TEC-less from −40 to +71 °C, 300 mW at 30 Hz. — [Jakobson et al., IISW 2025](https://imagesensors.org/Past%20Workshops/2025%20Workshop/2025%20Papers/P28_Jakobson.pdf)
- Responsivity always trades against time constant in microbolometers. A short-leg design reached τ ≈ 2.5 ms in simulation. — [Springer *Microsystem Technologies*, "Effect of beam length on the uncooled microbolometer performance" (2021)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00542-020-05192-y)
- A bolometer is always exposed and its signal decays exponentially, so it does not act as a snapshot imager and moving scenes smear. — [DiVA thesis, "Using high speed shutter to reduce…" (bachelor thesis)](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1021488/FULLTEXT02); 200 fps thermal capture showed "significant non-uniform motion blur" — [RSS 2020 paper](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss16/p022.pdf)
- Cooled MWIR frame rates: Blackbird 1920 up to 120 Hz full frame (4 video ports, 400 mW), 450 Hz windowed. Blackbird 1280 up to 180 Hz (600 Hz windowed). Blackbird 640 up to 350 Hz (1000 Hz windowed). — [Shkedy et al., OPTRO 2018](https://scdusa-ir.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/10µm-pitch-detectors-for-MWIR-applications_OPTRO-2018.pdf)

- Blackbird operability is >99.5 %, and SCD shows an uncorrected image with no bad-pixel replacement (BPR) to demonstrate it. — [Shkedy et al., OPTRO 2018](https://scdusa-ir.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/10µm-pitch-detectors-for-MWIR-applications_OPTRO-2018.pdf). ### Inferences
(Standard radiometry, sampling and TTP relations below are from my background knowledge of Holst, Driggers and Vollmerhausen texts. I did **not** re-verify them in a document fetched this session, so treat them as textbook relations to cross-check.)

- **IFOV and pitch.** IFOV = p/f. In the resolution-limited regime, range scales with 1/IFOV. For example, going from 15 µm to 10 µm at the same f is up to 1.5× range, but only if Fλ/d stays in the detector-limited zone (Section 2). The Nyquist frequency in object space is f_N = f/(2p) cy/rad.
- **Crosstalk as an MTF term (computed from SCD's numbers).** Treat the 56/9/2 % kernel as a discrete PSF. Its transfer function along x at Nyquist is 0.56 − 2(0.09) + 2(0.09) − 4(0.02) ≈ 0.48, multiplying the ideal aperture MTF sinc(0.5) ≈ 0.64 to give about 0.31. At half Nyquist the kernel gives ≈0.74 × 0.90 ≈ 0.67. The quoted "0.4 at half the Nyquist frequency" lies between these two values. Either way, at 10 µm pitch, crosstalk/diffusion is a *first-order* detector-MTF term, not a small correction, and users who enter only a geometric 100 %-fill-factor sinc will overestimate high-frequency MTF. Lynred's 0.48 (15 µm) and 0.40 (7.5 µm) at Nyquist support this: measured detector MTF at Nyquist is 0.3–0.5, versus 0.64 for an ideal square pixel.
- **NETD of a shot-noise/well-limited cooled FPA.** NETD ≈ √(N_e + σ_read²) / (N_e·C_th), where N_e is the integrated electrons and C_th is the relative thermal contrast (≈3.5–4 %/K for 3–5 µm at 300 K, a typical textbook value). Check against Blackbird-1920: N_e = 0.7 × 2.0 Me⁻ = 1.4 Me⁻ and σ_read = 330 e⁻ give a noise of ≈1228 e⁻, i.e. 8.8×10⁻⁴ relative. With C_th ≈ 3.9 %/K that is NETD ≈ 22.5 mK, matching the measured 22.5 mK mean. In this regime NETD is set by **well capacity × well fill** and hardly by integration time, F-number or QE, because those only change how fast the well fills. A larger well (4 Me⁻) or summing N frames (digital TDI) cuts NETD by about 1/√N. Lowering well fill from 70 % to 50 % raises NETD by about √(0.7/0.5) ≈ 1.18×, which is why NETD figures quoted at different well fills are not comparable.
- **Integration time and F-number when the well is *not* full** (dim scenes, short integration for platform motion, narrow XBn band). Background-limited NETD scales ≈ ∝ F/(p·√(η·t_int·Δλ-weighted flux)). Halving t_int raises NETD by √2. Going from F/3 to F/4 at fixed t_int raises NETD by ≈4/3 (BLIP) and shifts the optical cutoff 1/(λF) down. For uncooled detectors (detector-noise-limited), NETD ∝ (4F²+1), so F/1 → F/1.2 raises NETD by ≈1.38×.
- **Pitch and NETD (uncooled).** At fixed design, 17 → 12 µm halves pixel area (0.50×), which would roughly double NETD. Datasheets nonetheless show 12 µm cores at ≤20–50 mK, which means design improvements (absorber, thermal isolation, ROIC) more than made up the area loss.
- **Quantization.** With an LSB of about 25 mK (SCD bolometer), quantization noise ≈ 25/√12 ≈ 7.2 mK, which adds only ~0.5 mK in quadrature to a 50 mK NETD. 13–14-bit ADCs are not a range driver at nominal gain. They do matter when a wide-DR or high-temperature gain mode coarsens the LSB, e.g. the 1400 °C span mode.
- **Bolometer smear.** Blur extent in pixels ≈ ω·τ/IFOV. For a 10 °/s pan (174.5 mrad/s), τ = 10 ms and IFOV = 12 µm/50 mm = 0.24 mrad, the exponential smear has a 1/e length of about 7 pixels. In that case the motion MTF dominates identification range during slewing or on moving platforms, although static-scene range models ignore it.
- **TTP structure (Vollmerhausen et al. 2004/2008).** CTF_sys(ξ) = CTF_eye(ξ/SMAG)/MTF_sys(ξ) × [1 + α²·σ_n²(ξ)/L²]^½. Here σ_n is the eye/display-filtered noise (temporal noise reduced by frame rate × eye integration time, 3-D FPN not reduced), and aliasing is added to σ_n as target-dependent noise. TTP = ∫ (C_tgt/CTF_sys)^½ dξ over the band where C_tgt > CTF_sys. V = TTP·√A_tgt/R. P = (V/V50)^E / (1 + (V/V50)^E) with E ≈ 1.51 + 0.24·V/V50. Detector parameters therefore enter **only** through MTF_sys (pitch, aperture, crosstalk, smear), σ_n (NETD, 3-D noise, frame rate, aliasing) and the sampling term (pitch relative to optics cutoff).

## 2. First-order vs second-order drivers; noise-limited vs resolution-limited regimes; the Fλ/d (Q) metric

### Takeaway
DRI range is set first by IFOV (pitch/focal length) and system MTF, which bound resolution, and by NETD together with residual FPN, which bound contrast at long range or under low ΔT and poor atmosphere. Which of the two dominates is decided by Fλ/d. Holst places the detector-limited to optics-limited transition at about 0.41 < Fλ/d < 1.0, and aliasing vanishes at Fλ/d ≥ 2. Beyond that point, shrinking the pixel gives diminishing returns. Compared with scene, target and atmosphere uncertainty, detector-parameter errors are a modest (~30 %) contributor.

### Cited Findings
- Holst applied Fλ/d to Schade's equivalent resolution, Snyder's MTFA and the NVThermIP target-acquisition model. All three show performance moving from detector-limited to optics-limited somewhere in **0.41 < Fλ/d < 1.0**. Fλ/d is the ratio of detector cutoff to optics cutoff. — [Holst, "Imaging system performance based upon Fλ/d," Opt. Eng. 46(10), 103204 (2007), DOI 10.1117/1.2790066](https://proceedings.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/optical-engineering/volume-46/issue-10/103204/Imaging-system-performance-based-upon-F%ce%bbd/10.1117/1.2790066.full) (abstract via search). On aliasing, the search summary read "sampling aliasing does not occur when Fλ/d is below 2." This contradicts the sampling physics: optics cutoff ≤ Nyquist requires Fλ/d **≥** 2. I could not fetch the abstract to confirm the wording, so treat the "≥ 2" form as correct pending verification.
- Small Fλ/d means detector-limited operation, and in the sampling view the transition occurs at Fλ/d = 2. LWIR imaging systems typically come close to Fλ/d = 2. — [arXiv 2511.03868, "Midinfrared semiconductor photonics – a roadmap" (2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.03868) (secondary, quoting Holst/Driggers)
- Driggers et al.: smaller pixels stop helping range when the system is diffraction-limited, or when the small detector collects too few photons (noise-limited). — [Opt. Eng. 51(6), 063202 (2012)](https://photonicsforenergy.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/optical-engineering/volume-51/issue-6/063202/Infrared-detector-size-how-low-should-you-go/10.1117/1.OE.51.6.063202.full)
- Minimum useful pitch (Holst & Driggers): 2 µm MWIR / 5 µm LWIR at F/1 and high SNR; 3 µm / 6 µm at F/1.2 and lower SNR. — [Opt. Eng. 51(9), 096401 (2012)](https://spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/Optical-Engineering/volume-51/issue-9/096401/Small-detectors-in-infrared-system-design/10.1117/1.OE.51.9.096401.pdf)
- Error-budget perspective (Perić et al. 2019, Table 5, estimated inaccuracies): scene signature 50–250 %, target properties up to 200 %, atmosphere ~100 %, display/observer ~100 %, observation time up to 200 %, **imager parameters ~30 %**. Weather and transmission losses "cannot be overcome by better imager technology," and better models do not substantially improve field predictions. — [Perić et al., Sensors 19(15), 3313 (2019)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6696215/)
- Same paper, Table 7: NVThermIP standard-weather ranges vs pure geometric (pixel-count) ranges. SYS1 (MWIR 640×512/15 µm, f = 330 mm, F/4) human detection: 9.1 km vs 13.9 km. SYS2 (MWIR 1280×1024/15 µm, f = 1200 mm, F/4.7) human detection: 18.1 km vs 50 km. SYS3 (LWIR 640×480/17 µm, f = 225 mm, F/1.5) human detection: 4.3 km vs 6.3 km. The reduction is largest for the long-focal-length (diffraction-/atmosphere-limited) SYS2, about −64 %. Ranges derived from lab MRTD fell between the standard- and bad-weather predictions in most cases. — [Perić et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6696215/) (percentages computed from the tables. The fetch tool also flagged that it could not reproduce the paper's geometric "G" column from Eq. 10, e.g. ~9.9 km vs 13.9 km for SYS1 human detection, so use G values with caution.)
- SCD's application analysis: for short integration time (fast airborne), spectral bandwidth (InSb full band vs XBn blue band) becomes the limiting SNR factor. For long-range observation, the blue band's better atmospheric transmission and diffraction MTF increase DRI range. — [Shkedy et al., OPTRO 2018](https://scdusa-ir.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/10µm-pitch-detectors-for-MWIR-applications_OPTRO-2018.pdf)

### Inferences
- **First-order (usually change range by tens of %):** pitch/IFOV (with focal length), detector MTF including crosstalk (at Nyquist 0.3–0.5 vs 0.64 ideal), NETD at the *actual* operating F#/t_int/well fill, and residual FPN when it is comparable to temporal noise (e.g. uncooled cores with shutterless NUC drift, or cooled FPAs far from their NUC calibration points). Also spectral band, through atmospheric transmission × thermal contrast at long range.
- **Second-order (usually <10 % under nominal conditions):** QE, dark current and read noise once the FPA is BLIP/well-limited (SCD's 22.5 mK matches the pure well-fill shot-noise limit, so read noise adds only ~4 %). Also ADC bit depth (≥13 bits), frame rate (beyond eye integration benefit), operability >99.5 % with BPR, and 1/f noise for modern ROICs.
- **Scenario-dependent, potentially first-order:** bolometer time constant (motion and slewing), integration time limited by platform motion (cooled), HOT-temperature dark current at the upper operating limit, NUC drift with ambient temperature, and binning/TDI in photon-starved SWIR/VIS (low light).
- **Regime rule of thumb (computed).** MWIR at λ ≈ 4 µm: F/4 with 15 µm gives Fλ/d ≈ 1.07, 10 µm gives 1.6, and Lynred's 7.5 µm at F/5.5 gives 2.9. LWIR at λ ≈ 10 µm: F/1 with 17 µm gives 0.59 and 12 µm gives 0.83. So uncooled LWIR is still detector-limited and gains strongly from smaller pitch. Cooled MWIR at 10 µm/F/4 is already near or past the transition, so further pitch reduction gains mostly FOV/format (or SWaP) rather than per-target resolution, unless F# is lowered.
- **Noise-limited vs resolution-limited in TTP.** If C_tgt ≫ CTF_sys over the whole MTF passband, range scales almost linearly with 1/IFOV (resolution-limited). If CTF_sys crosses C_tgt well below the MTF cutoff (low ΔT, long path, high NETD/FPN), the TTP integral is truncated by noise, so NETD/FPN improvements give range gains and pitch reductions give little.

### Gaps
- No published numeric sensitivity (e.g. ∂R/∂NETD or ∂R/∂p in % per %) from TRM4 or NVIPM for a reference system was retrieved. Such a sensitivity study would need to be computed with the models themselves.
- Fiete's λFN/p analysis for remote sensing *(foundational, 1999)* was not retrieved.

## 3. TRM4 vs NVIPM/TTP treatment of detector noise and sampling; published comparisons

### Takeaway
TRM4 handles sampling deterministically: it uses the 4-bar AMOP at the optimum phase, so bars above Nyquist can still count. It then converts MTDP/MCP to ranges with modified Johnson / STANAG 4347 criteria, with temporal and non-uniformity noise included. NVIPM integrates target contrast above a noise-raised CTF (TTP) and treats aliasing as target-dependent noise. I found no peer-reviewed head-to-head TRM4-vs-NVIPM range benchmark. Indirect evidence (NVThermIP vs TOD) shows inter-model differences of about 25 % for undersampled imagers.

### Cited Findings
- **TRM4 (Fraunhofer IOSB).** Analytical model for visible to thermal IR, developed and validated for well- and undersampled imagers. Sensor sampling is simulated "rigorously" through AMOP, whereas conventional MRTD-based models are "restricted to well sampled imagers." Ranges come from 4-bar perception with the STANAG 4347 approach. — [ECOMOS TRM page](https://ecomos.org/information/trm/); [IOSB TRM4 page](https://www.iosb.fraunhofer.de/en/projects-and-products/trm4.html)
- TRM4 versions: v1 2010–11, v1.1 2013, **v2 (June 2016) added uncooled thermal imagers**, v3 (Sept 2021) added iCCD/iCMOS, NVG, direct-view and MCP for VIS–SWIR, and v4 (Aug 2026) added colour cameras (DOI 10.1117/12.3070119). IOSB validates by comparing measured and calculated MTDP. Measured MTDP values can be fed straight into the range calculation. — [IOSB TRM4 page](https://www.iosb.fraunhofer.de/en/projects-and-products/trm4.html); ["Imager performance assessment with TRM4 version 3: an overview," Proc. SPIE 11866 (2021), DOI 10.1117/12.2597723](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/11866/2597723/Imager-performance-assessment-with-TRM4-version-3-an-overview/10.1117/12.2597723.short)
- TRM4 "includes a validated approach to aliasing in range performance assessment of focal plane arrays" — ["Imager performance assessment with TRM4: recent developments," Proc. SPIE 12533, 125330P (2023), DOI 10.1117/12.2668884](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/12533/125330P/Imager-performance-assessment-with-TRM4-recent-developments/10.1117/12.2668884.short); intensified-camera module accounting for aliasing of sampled imagers — [Proc. SPIE 11001, 1100109 (2019), DOI 10.1117/12.2518317](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/11001/1100109/An-intensified-camera-module-for-the-range-performance-model-TRM4/10.1117/12.2518317.short) (abstract-level info via search)
- **NVIPM/TTP.** Aliased signal is treated as target-dependent noise, added in quadrature to detector noise, with a penalty that grows with range. — [Vollmerhausen, Driggers, Wilson, JOSA A 25(8), 2055 (2008)](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/predicting-range-performance-of-sampled-imagers-by-treating-alias/); follow-up "Performance assessment of treating aliased signal as target-dependent noise" (Preece & Haefner, SPIE 2011), named in search results but not retrieved.

- Indirect inter-model comparison: NVThermIP predicted about **25 % longer ranges than the TOD model** for undersampled imagers under the stated conditions. — ["NVThermIP vs TOD: matching the target acquisition range criteria"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/255630287_NVThermIP_vs_TOD_matching_the_target_acquisition_range_criteria) (via search snippet; authors/year not confirmed)

- Pilotage-TTP variant: noise degrades the eye CTF by a noise factor, and FPN impact depends on scene motion (DMRT vs MRT). — [Proc. SPIE 13468, 134680B (2025)](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/13468/134680B/The-impact-of-fixed-pattern-noise-on-moving-imagery-as/10.1117/12.3054006.full)

### Inferences
- **Sampling.** TRM4's AMOP is computed for the 4-bar pattern at the *best* phase and averaged over bars. It therefore credits sub-Nyquist-to-beyond-Nyquist bar perception and gives undersampled FPAs (high Fλ/d deficit, large pitch) relatively optimistic high-frequency performance compared with a Nyquist-cut MRTD. NVIPM instead keeps the full pre-sample MTF and charges aliasing as noise. For low-contrast, long-range targets it therefore penalises undersampled systems more as range grows. The two models are thus expected to diverge most for low-Fλ/d systems (uncooled LWIR at F/1 with 12–17 µm, Fλ/d ≈ 0.6–0.8), and least for well-sampled systems (Fλ/d ≥ 2).
- **Noise.** Both models use temporal noise (NETD) and spatial/non-uniformity noise. NVIPM takes the full 3-D noise set (σtvh, σtv, σth, σvh, σv, σh, σt), filtered by eye/display, with spatial terms not averaged by frame integration. TRM4's public pages list "temporal and non-uniformity noise" without exposing the 3-D decomposition. Users with measured 3-D noise must map σvh/σv/σh into TRM4's non-uniformity input (see Gaps).
- **Criteria.** TRM4 uses modified Johnson (line-pair) criteria via STANAG 4347. NVIPM uses V50 (TTP cycles). The two are not numerically interchangeable, so detector changes that alter MTF *shape* above Nyquist (crosstalk, smear) will affect TTP-based ranges more than Johnson-based ranges.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed numerical TRM4-vs-NVIPM comparison for the same system was found. The "TRM4 component for NV-IPM" paper abstract was blocked (HTTP 403).
- The exact TRM4 MTDP/MDSP noise equation (how σ_temporal and σ_FPN are combined, eye integration time, the SNR threshold constant) was not retrievable from public IOSB/ECOMOS pages. The IOSB page refers inquiries to trm-info@iosb.fraunhofer.de and to the v4 publication (DOI 10.1117/12.3070119).
- Whether TRM4 v2+ models microbolometer time-constant smear explicitly was not confirmed.

## 5. Parameters commonly hidden/fixed in simplified range calculators, and common user input errors

### Takeaway
Simplified calculators (pixel-count/Johnson "geometric" ranges) usually take only pitch, format and focal length. They hide or fix NETD, detector MTF/crosstalk, FPN, F#, well fill and smear, and can overstate range by 30–70 % compared with NVThermIP-type predictions. The most common input errors are NETD quoted at non-matching conditions (F#, well fill, frame rate, temperature), using an ideal 100 % fill-factor pixel MTF, ignoring residual FPN, and mixing pixel-based criteria with line-pair criteria.

### Cited Findings
- Geometric (pixel-count) ranges are optimistic: an imager "cannot 'see' farther than its geometrical range." Standard NVThermIP predictions fell 32–64 % below the geometric values for detection in the three example systems, and lab-MRTD-based ranges 17–74 % below. The difference is largest for diffraction-limited ultra-long-range systems. — [Perić et al., Sensors 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6696215/)
- Calculators and datasheets use different task criteria: pixel criteria D/R/I = 2/7/13 pixels vs Johnson 1/4/6.4 line pairs (≈2/8/12.8 pixels), with standard assumptions of ΔT = 2 K and P = 50 %. — [Perić et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6696215/)
- Johnson single-frequency metrics ignore digital processing and sub-limiting-frequency behaviour, so they do not fully describe FPA imagers. — [Perić et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6696215/)
- NETD in manufacturer data is specified under different, non-standard conditions: 70 % well fill (Blackbird 1920/1280, Mini Blackbird), 50 % well fill (Sparrow 640), 60 % well fill (SCD Figure 7 comparisons), F/3 vs F/4, and F/1, 60 Hz, 25 °C for uncooled. — [SCD Blackbird 1920](https://scdusa-ir.com/products/blackbird-1920); [Sparrow 640](https://scdusa-ir.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Sparrow_Blackbird-640_updated-V2a.pdf); [Shkedy 2018](https://scdusa-ir.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/10µm-pitch-detectors-for-MWIR-applications_OPTRO-2018.pdf); [Jakobson 2025](https://imagesensors.org/Past%20Workshops/2025%20Workshop/2025%20Papers/P28_Jakobson.pdf). For Boson/Boson+, NETD is tiered by product grade (≤20/≤30 mK; <40/<50/<60 mK) — [Teledyne FLIR](https://oem.flir.com/en-ca/products/boson/).
- RNU depends on well fill and on distance from the NUC calibration points (20/80 %). It also varies with operating time and ambient temperature. SCD shows global RNU changes after a NUC performed at 20 °C when the ambient is 0–50 °C. — [Shkedy et al., OPTRO 2018](https://scdusa-ir.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/10µm-pitch-detectors-for-MWIR-applications_OPTRO-2018.pdf)
- FPN has a different perceptual effect on moving vs static imagery (DMRT vs MRT, a 2–3× difference in FPN impact), so a single static FPN input can misstate range for moving-platform tasks. — [Proc. SPIE 13468, 134680B (2025)](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/13468/134680B/The-impact-of-fixed-pattern-noise-on-moving-imagery-as/10.1117/12.3054006.full)
- In TRM4, users can bypass most detector inputs by entering measured MTDP directly. Correct use then depends on how and where MTDP was measured (IOSB publishes its MTDP measurement method). — [IOSB TRM4 page](https://www.iosb.fraunhofer.de/en/projects-and-products/trm4.html)
- Leonardo DRS has published a white paper on misleading range data, "The truth about range data" (2017). — [Leonardo DRS PDF](https://www.leonardodrs.com/media/10494/201705_truth_about_rangedata_mr-2014-10-683_rev02.pdf) (found in search, **not fetched**; contents unverified)

### Inferences
Likely user errors, each with its approximate range consequence:
1. **NETD at the wrong conditions.** Typical cases: entering a datasheet NETD at 70 % WF while the scenario's integration time gives 20–30 % WF (NETD ↑ ~1.5–1.9×); entering an F/1 bolometer NETD for an F/1.2–1.4 lens (×1.38–1.93 via (4F²+1)); or entering a 30 Hz NETD for a 60 Hz mode. All three overstate range in noise-limited cases.
2. **Ideal detector MTF.** Using sinc with 100 % fill factor and no diffusion/crosstalk term overstates MTF at Nyquist by about 1.3–2× for 7.5–10 µm III-V FPAs (0.64 ideal vs 0.31–0.48 measured). This mostly inflates recognition/identification ranges.
3. **FPN set to zero or to the factory RNU at the calibration point.** Field RNU after ambient drift or shutterless operation can approach or exceed temporal noise. Because FPN is not averaged by the eye over frames, this hurts low-ΔT detection most.
4. **Integration time unconstrained by platform motion.** Cooled imagers on fast platforms may need short t_int, so they are not at the nominal WF.
5. **Ignoring bolometer τ.** Static-scene models omit motion smear (ω·τ/IFOV pixels), which can be several pixels during slewing.
6. **Mixing criteria.** Pixel D/R/I = 2/7/13 vs Johnson lp 1/4/6.4 vs TTP V50, entered with the wrong model.
7. **Format confused with resolution.** A larger format at the same pitch and focal length increases FOV, not per-target range.
8. **Pitch reduced while F# held fixed.** In the optics-limited zone (Fλ/d > ~1), range gain is much smaller than the pitch ratio.
9. **Wrong spectral band or QE** for HOT vs full-band detectors (XBn 4.2 µm cut-off vs InSb 4.9 µm; T2SL 5.1 µm). This changes both signal and atmospheric transmission.
10. **Treating operability/BPR, bit depth and dark current as range drivers.** They rarely are once the FPA is BLIP and well-limited.
